What makes a project grid-friendly

Accepts limited access

Agrees to limited or flexible grid access, including interruption when the grid is tight

Uses free capacity

Shifts consumption or production to hours when the grid has room

Right profile, right place

Its load profile and location fit the local grid

Supports safe operation

Its technical behaviour helps keep the grid secure and robust

“Projects that burden the grid less can get a better place in the queue relative to other projects in the same category.”

Political agreement on the grid emergency plan, 1 October 2026, p. 5 (our translation)

Source: Political agreement on the grid emergency plan, 1 October 2026, pp. 4–5 (criteria summarised)

07

The political agreement lists four things Energinet and the grid companies are expected to weigh when they judge grid-friendliness. One: does the project accept limited or flexible grid access, including being interrupted? Two: does it use the grid when there is room and hold back when capacity is tight? Three: do its load profile and location suit the local grid? Four: does its technical behaviour support safe and robust grid operation? The detailed methods must be approved by the Danish energy regulator, and grid operators can start with their existing interruptible products right away. The quote at the bottom is the key sentence for us: projects that burden the grid less move up the queue within their category.
