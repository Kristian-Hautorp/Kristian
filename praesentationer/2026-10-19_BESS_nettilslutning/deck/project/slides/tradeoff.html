Our choice

The trade-off we accept

What we give up

Lower maximum output: less room for short price peaks and fast grid services

The grid operator may curtail the battery when the grid is congested

What we gain

A stronger, grid-friendly application under the new rules

A better chance of an earlier grid connection

More stored energy per day: 4 hours instead of 2

Lower grid connection costs expected for a curtailable connection

Our view: a battery that gets connected is worth more than a more powerful one waiting in the queue.

Qualitative assessment by the project team. Financial impact to be quantified separately.

12

We want to be transparent about the trade-off. With lower power, the battery can capture less of the short, sharp price peaks and offer less capacity to fast grid services. And with limited grid access, the grid operator may curtail the battery when the grid is congested. In return we get an application that fits the new rules much better, a better chance of being connected earlier, twice the storage duration, and we expect lower grid connection costs for a curtailable connection. In a market where grid access is the scarce resource, time to connection matters more than peak power. A battery that is connected and earning is worth more than a more powerful one that waits in the queue. The financial impact is being quantified separately; this slide is a qualitative assessment.
