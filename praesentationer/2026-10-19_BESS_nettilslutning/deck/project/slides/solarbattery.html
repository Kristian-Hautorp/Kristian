This is the simple idea behind our design. A solar park produces most of its power around midday, and its grid connection is used only during daylight. When the battery sits next to the solar park and shares the same connection, two things happen. At midday, solar power above the connection limit goes into the battery instead of being curtailed. In the evening, when the solar park produces little or nothing, the battery delivers that power through the same connection. The grid connection is used for more hours of the day, without asking the grid for extra capacity. That is precisely the kind of ‘better use of the existing grid’ the new Danish rules are designed to reward. The chart is illustrative only; it is not based on project data.
