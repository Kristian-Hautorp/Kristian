06

The new law sorts every large grid request into four lanes. Lane 1 protects households, existing customers and critical functions. Lane 2 is the green transition, including renewable production such as our solar parks. Lane 3 is energy storage: batteries, and also storage capacity in combined plants that goes beyond the plant’s ordinary production or consumption needs. Lane 4 is mainly data centres. Two points matter for us. First, inside lanes 2 to 4, grid-friendliness decides the order, and a storage project that helps the grid make room for more connections can be ranked higher. Second, grid operators may now refuse lane 3 and 4 requests where no capacity is expected. For batteries, being grid-friendly is therefore not a nice-to-have; it decides whether you get in at all.
